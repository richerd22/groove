The runout groove: what the etchings mean

The short version: The smooth band between the last song and the label is called the dead wax or runout, and it carries the record's paperwork: a matrix number for each side, cut numbers, stamper codes, plant marks and often the initials of the engineer who cut it.100 Read the right way, those scratches can tell you which factory pressed your copy and which lacquer it came from, but no single mark proves a first pressing on its own.100101 And every so often there is a joke in there too.102

What is the dead wax, and why is anything written there?#

The matrix number is a code, letters and numbers and sometimes symbols, stamped or handwritten in the runout groove area, which also goes by dead wax, end-groove area or matrix area.100 It exists for the factory, to file the stamper and make sure each side gets the right label. Collectors borrowed it later to tell editions apart.100 Around that core number you can find a cut or take number, plant codes or logos, the cutting engineer's initials or signature, and sometimes dates.100

Don't confuse it with the catalog number, the bigger number that is usually the same on both labels. Matrix numbers differ side to side, and some labels print them upside down to avoid exactly that confusion.100

There is a geography to it. On British records the lacquer number normally sits at 6 o'clock, with matrix and stamper information 90 degrees away at 9, 12 or 3 o'clock.103 Run a fingernail over them: stamper codes are embossed and stand proud of the surface, while lacquer and mother codes are carved in.103 On Contemporary Records' jazz runouts, the style of the stamps tracks the plating plant and the era, not the cutting room.104

The habit is old. Before about 1950, records were cut direct to disc and take numbers were inscribed in the matrix area; on Victor records the take number sits at "9 o'clock".100 In the 1950s, one engineer recalls, every cutter at Fine Sound had a personal "maker's mark" that he inscribed, and dedicated mastering houses later stamped their own imprints.105

What do A1, B1, -1A and RE actually mean?#

The number after the dash is usually the cut. A suffix such as 12345-3 marks the third cut of that side, and an album numbered X-1234 might carry X-1234-B7 in the runout, the 7 being the cut number.100 Records get recut for ordinary reasons: stampers wear out or get damaged, a test pressing is rejected, a fault turns up in the banding, another plant or country cuts its own masters, or a reissue gets a new number.100 Sometimes the old cut is reused with the new number added and the old one scratched out.100

Vinyl Me, Please says a "-1A" suffix "typically" indicates a first pressing of side A, and "RE" a repressing.106 A UK shop blog lists "A1 B1" (also written A//1 B//1 or A-1 B-1) as a common first-press code.107 Both are good starting points, and neither is a rule. The clearest proof comes from inside Columbia.

A former Columbia cutting engineer, Phil Brown, told AnalogPlanet that an initial lacquer order was six sets: 1A and 1B went to the Pitman plant, 1C and 1D to Terre Haute, 1E and 1F to Santa Maria.101 All six are first cuttings, so the collector's belief that A came first, B second and C third is wrong.101 Each plant got two sets because lacquers could be lost in plating. A change of program moved the number to -2, then -3, and if the first lacquer was ruined, the next cut could be 2A and be treated as the first.101

Two more cautions: a cut 1 may have been rejected, and reissues often restart at 1.100 A "-RE" suffix doesn't necessarily mean the non-RE version was ever sold to the public.100 Letters can mean people, too. In a lacquer number such as "AM 438-1", a letter suffix like -1R can identify the mastering engineer at that firm.103

How do stamper codes like GRAMOPHLTD and BUCKINGHAM work?#

Two big British companies hid their stamper counts in words. At Decca the code word was BUCKINGHAM: B=1, U=2, C=3, K=4, I=5, N=6, G=7, H=8, A=9, M=0, so "NN" means the 66th stamper.103 EMI's Hayes plant used GRAMOPHLTD: G=1, R=2, A=3, M=4, O=5, P=6, H=7, L=8, T=9, D=0, so "GG" is the 11th stamper.103 A low stamper code means your copy came off early metal, which is as close to "early copy" as the dead wax gets.

Code word Company Letter to digit Example
BUCKINGHAM Decca (UK) B1 U2 C3 K4 I5 N6 G7 H8 A9 M0 NN = 66th stamper
GRAMOPHLTD EMI Hayes (UK) G1 R2 A3 M4 O5 P6 H7 L8 T9 D0 GG = 11th stamper

EMI's layout puts the mother number at 9 o'clock, the stamper G-code at 3 o'clock, and a pre-1981 example string "EMI 5499 A-1-1-1-4".108 The Gramophone Co. used the same system with a tax code, "KT", at 12 o'clock.108 CBS at Aston Clinton in the UK put the matrix at 12, the stamper at 3 and the mother letter at 9.108

On EMI records, YEX and YAX prefixes usually mean stereo and XEX usually mono, and "1Y//1 2Y//1" often marks an early pressing.107

Who signed my record?#

The most famous name in the dead wax belongs to George Peckham, who started cutting at Apple in London in 1968 and signed his work "Porky" or "A Porky Prime Cut".102 He also used "Pecko Duck", "Delta Pork" and similar variations.102 His notes were often funny or cryptic, nodding to the artist or the lyrics, sometimes with a small drawing; Sound & Vision compares the habit to programmers leaving notes in source code.102 He left a great many hidden runout messages, and may have cut hundreds of thousands of records, mostly UK pressings.100

In the US the marks are usually initials. RL is Robert (Bob) Ludwig, BG Bernie Grundman and KG Kevin Gray.106109 RVG marks classic jazz cuts by Rudy Van Gelder.110109 At Masterdisk, HW is Howie Weinberg and GK Gilbert Kong; LH is Lee Hulko and TJ Ted Jensen, both at Sterling Sound.111 Wally is Wally Traugott at Capitol.110111

Some are pictures. Collectors read bird wings, sometimes swapped for a small flower, as Brian Gardner at Allen Zentz Mastering, and "eyebrows" as David Turner.110 A cent sign is Tom Coyne, a pun on his name.112 In the UK, HTM is Harry T. Moss at EMI and Abbey Road, found on records including Revolver and The Dark Side of the Moon.112

Initials collide, so read them in context. "AT/GP" looks like Peckham but is George Piros at Atlantic Studios in New York - a mix-up one collector made in print and fixed after a reader wrote in.110 Piros wrote P plus a number, the number naming a specific lathe and cutter-head combination; P13 and P17 both turn up.105113 And "RE" can be a repress marker or a set of initials, as for Rick Essig at Frankford/Wayne.106114

What do STERLING, IAM and the other plant marks mean?#

Mastering houses often stamped their names. STERLING, stamped or etched, is Sterling Sound in New York.106 A "Mastered by MasterDisc" stamp marks Masterdisk.105 TOWNHOUSE and STRAWBERRY are London rooms.112 Artisan Sound Recorders in Los Angeles used a logo that Vinyl Me, Please describes as a drum with two drumsticks and a collector describes as a drafting compass on a record, the compass forming an "A".106110

Pressing plants used letters and symbols. On Capitol records, IAM in a triangle points to the Scranton, Pennsylvania plant, roughly 1963 to 1973; IAM is the International Association of Machinists, the union.106110 A rifle- or arrow-like mark is Capitol's Winchester, Virginia plant, and a five- or six-point star is Capitol Los Angeles.108 Columbia's three US plants show up as P (Pitman, New Jersey), T (Terre Haute, Indiana) and S (Santa Maria, California), with variants.108101 RCA used a stamped H, I or R for Hollywood, Indianapolis and Rockaway.115 MR in a circle marks Monarch in Los Angeles.110108

Not every symbol is a signature. A triangle followed by five digits, the delta number, is a job code used by Monarch and other plants, so it doesn't prove Monarch by itself.116108 Allied Record Company shows up as "K-###" in one plant list and as a Q-shaped stamp in another.108110 The decoder lists more than 100 of these marks and flags the disputed ones.

How do European plants mark their records?#

European plants have their own dialects. Optimal Media in Röbel, Germany, uses an inverted 1 and a downward triangle; Pallas in Diepholz puts four or five digits between hyphens; Sonopress in Gütersloh pairs a three-digit date with an F-number, so "107 F-2654" means July 1991.108 CBS in Haarlem, the Netherlands, used strings like "OI_7261_IA_I" from 1977 to 1987, and Ljudpress in Östersund, Sweden, etched LP, sometimes with a date.108 In the UK, a six-point asterisk is Audio Services in Wednesbury.108 "P USA" is Pallas pressing for US export.106

What does DMM mean in the dead wax?#

DMM, Direct Metal Mastering, means the record was cut straight into a copper-plated master rather than a lacquer.54 Teldec and Georg Neumann GmbH developed it, Neumann building the cutters into its VMS80 lathe series, and the idea came from RCA's late-1970s videodisc work.54 The payoff is in the factory: one electroplating stage to make stampers instead of three for lacquer, and in the early 1980s many copies could be pressed from one master without wear-driven recuts.54100 You will see "DMM" on the sleeve or inscribed in the lead-out area.54

The sound is argued about; some listeners hear DMM pressings as harsh or forward in the treble.54

Only about 30 DMM lathes were made, and about 10 of those were converted to lacquer. As of 2009, seven publicly usable DMM rooms remained, in Germany, the Netherlands and the Czech Republic, and the US had lost its last in 2005.54 Masterdisk's user-written Discogs profile tells a different story: DMM cutting since February 1986, still listed among its services.117 Both can't be current, and nobody has settled which is.

What are the famous runout messages?#

Some copies of Led Zeppelin's "Immigrant Song" single carry "Do What Thou Wilt Shall Be The Whole Of The Law", believed to have been requested by the band.100 U2's Rattle and Hum (1988) reads "We Love You A.L.K.", for production manager Anne Louise Kelly.100 Elvis Costello's This Year's Model in the UK (1978) had a contest message with a phone number, and Emitt Rhodes' self-titled album says "Recorded at Home" in ornate lettering.100 The practice survived the format change: most US CDs of Happy Mondays' Pills 'n' Thrills and Bellyaches say "CALL THE COPS".100

Neil Young's Tonight's the Night says "Goodbye Waterface", and Third Man Records puts a message on every release.106

How do I use all this to identify my pressing?#

  1. Copy the runout exactly. Discogs asks contributors to enter it verbatim, one string per side, keeping capitalization, single spaces for gaps and strikethrough for crossed-out text.118 Then search Discogs by the full string.109
  2. Sort raised from carved. Embossed codes come from the stamper; carved ones from the lacquer or mother.103
  3. Find the plant. A letter or logo usually does it. If there is none, measure the pressing ring on the label, which differs by plant: about 70mm at Specialty, 36mm at Capitol Winchester, a double 35/72mm at Monarch.110
  4. Look for the lowest cut and stamper numbers. The lowest indicators, such as -A, -1 or -1S, point to earlier copies.109 Keep the Columbia caveat in mind: 1A to 1F were all first cuttings sent to different plants.101
  5. Check the paper too. Label designs date a pressing: the Warner Bros. green label ran from about 1970 to 1973, then the "Burbank" palm-tree label from 1973 to 1978.110 A barcode on a 1965 title means a reissue, since barcodes were not widely used before the late 1970s.109
  6. Cross-check. Discogs has errors, so check forums and specialist sites as well.110

On CDs the same instinct applies with different codes. Discogs separates the L-prefixed mastering SID, added at glass mastering, from the IFPI mould SID added at pressing, and notes that an "LC" code is a label code, not a matrix or catalog number.118

Quick answers

What do the numbers and letters in the dead wax of a record mean?
They are production codes: a matrix number for each side, a cut number, and often stamper codes, plant marks and the cutting engineer's initials. The factory uses them to file stampers and match labels; collectors use them to identify editions.
What does "Porky Prime Cut" mean on a record?
It is the signature of George Peckham, a UK cutting engineer who started at Apple in London in 1968. His runout notes were often jokes or nods to the artist.
What does the IAM triangle mean on a Capitol record?
It marks Capitol's Scranton, Pennsylvania plant, roughly 1963 to 1973. IAM stands for the International Association of Machinists, the union.
What does DMM mean on a vinyl record?
Direct Metal Mastering: the record was cut straight into a copper-plated master instead of a lacquer. It needs one plating stage instead of three, and some listeners describe the sound as bright in the treble.
