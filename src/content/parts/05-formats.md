## Sizes, speeds, weights and colors

**The short version:** A 12 inch at 33 1/3 rpm holds about 18 to 22 minutes a side by one plant's guidance, with longer sides possible depending on the music; spin the same disc at 45 and you trade minutes for a faster groove.[^prs-gotta-groove-mastering][^prs-hifinews] A 180 gram record uses the same stampers and groove depth as a standard one of about 140 grams, and hi-fi makers disagree on whether the weight itself changes the sound.[^prs-discmakers-weight][^prs-whathifi] Colored, splatter and picture discs are made differently from black and can be noisier, and the new PET "eco" records come with maker claims nobody has yet tested independently.[^prs-hand-drawn-faq][^prs-fvtp]

[[DIAGRAM-IDEA: Two 12 inch discs side by side, one at 33 1/3 and one at 45, with arrows showing groove speed at the outer edge (about 21 vs about 28 inches per second) and a shaded inner zone where groove speed falls.]]

### Why does a 45 sound different from a 33?

Because the groove is moving faster under the stylus. On a 12 inch at 33 1/3 rpm, the groove passes the stylus at "almost 21ips" (inches per second) at the outer edge and about 7.4 ips near the label; at 45 rpm the outer edge runs about 28.3 ips, which HiFi News notes is "close to the professional tape standard of 30ips."[^prs-hifinews] HiFi News says higher-speed formats are "almost always sonically superior."[^prs-hifinews] The cost is time.

Groove speed also falls toward the center of every record, so high frequencies get harder to reproduce there; one guide suggests sequencing with that in mind and notes how many sides end with a ballad.[^prs-soundfly]

| Speed | Typical sizes | Groove speed at outer edge (12 inch) | Plant guidance on side length |
|---|---|---|---|
| 33 1/3 rpm | 12 inch LP; 7 inch with caution | about 21 ips | 12 inch: 18 min ideal, 20 "still good," issues may start at 22 (one US plant); definition drops above about 20 (a Danish plant)[^prs-gotta-groove-mastering][^prs-rpm-length] |
| 45 rpm | 7 inch; 12 inch | about 28.3 ips | 12 inch: 12 min ideal, 14 OK (one US plant), up to 15 (the Danish plant); 7 inch: 4.5 min (US plant), up to 5 (Danish plant)[^prs-gotta-groove-mastering][^prs-rpm-length] |
| 78 rpm | 7, 10, 12 and 16 inch (shellac)[^gf-b-psap] | faster than 45; no figure in our sources | three to four minutes a side (an archive's figure)[^gf-b-nfsa] |

The 78 came before vinyl. Shellac 78s were made commercially from 1897 until the late 1950s, from shellac resin with fillers such as clay, slate and limestone plus carbon black and cotton fibers, and their use declined steadily after World War II.[^gf-b-psap] Early 78s were often not cut at exactly 78 rpm, so the right speed can vary from disc to disc.[^gf-b-psap]

The plants disagree at the edges, and the music matters more than the clock: a quiet chamber recording can stretch a 33 side to 25-27 minutes, which one mastering engineer calls "almost no problem," while heavy sibilance or limiting makes long sides harder to cut cleanly.[^prs-rpm-length][^prs-gotta-groove-mastering] For a 7 inch, one plant allows 6 minutes at 33 1/3 but prefers 45 when the song fits; the Danish plant does not recommend 33 on a 7 inch at all.[^prs-gotta-groove-mastering][^prs-rpm-length] [How a record is cut](/cutting/) explains the trade-off between loudness and length.

### What are the standard sizes?

| Size | Usual speed | Typical weight | Side length (plant guidance) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 7 inch | 45 (33 1/3 possible) | about 40 grams or more | 4.5-5 min at 45[^prs-gotta-groove-mastering][^prs-rpm-length] |
| 10 inch | 33 1/3 or 45 | about 110 grams (one plant) | up to 15 min at 33; up to 10 min at 45 (one plant)[^gf-b-standardvinyl] |
| 12 inch | 33 1/3 or 45 | about 140 grams standard; 180 grams "heavyweight" | 18-22 min at 33; 12-15 min at 45 |

The 7 and 12 inch weights are from Disc Makers and Gotta Groove.[^prs-discmakers-weight][^prs-gotta-groove-faq] Plants press all three sizes; one Nashville plant lists 7, 10 and 12 inch output.[^prs-urp-process]

### Why do some records say 180 gram?

A **180 gram** record is a heavier 12 inch LP. Standard weight is "around 140 grams" per Disc Makers, or "between 130 and 155 grams" per Gotta Groove.[^prs-discmakers-weight][^prs-gotta-groove-faq] Final weight also shifts with the vinyl color and the master content.[^prs-discmakers-weight][^prs-gotta-groove-faq]

What weight does not change: 180 gram records use the same stampers and the same groove depth as standard weight, and Disc Makers says that on most systems they sound the same.[^prs-discmakers-weight] Whether that settles it depends on whom you ask. In a 2025 What Hi-Fi? survey of turntable and cartridge makers:[^prs-whathifi]

- **The "it's the cut, not the weight" side.** Rega's Simon Webster said groove depth is set by the cutting engineer and "you can have excellent 180g pressings and poor 180g pressings." DUAL's Lothar Mertens said 120 gram and 180 gram copies from the same master can sound identical. Clearaudio's Robert Suchy said heavy vinyl "can't make a poor recording sound good."
- **The "weight can help" side.** Audio-Technica's Edward Forth said less vibration reaches the stylus, and Suchy said resonance may be lower. Webster said heavier records are more durable and less prone to warp.
- **The caveats.** Forth said a warped 180 gram disc cannot be restored the way a 140 gram one can. Ortofon's Leif Johannsen said thicker discs need careful cooling after pressing or they warp. Technics' Tetsuya Itani said, from personal experience, that they may pick up more static.

The weight is printed on the sticker; the quality of the cut and the pressing is not.

### Does colored vinyl sound worse than black?

**Colored** records are pressed from colored PVC pellets, and black gets its color from carbon black, which also strengthens the record; colored compound uses dyes and other additives instead.[^prs-vinylmoon][^prs-vinylroom] A Dallas plant's FAQ says colored and picture discs "can introduce higher surface noise if not handled properly," and that black virgin vinyl "typically remains the lowest-noise option."[^prs-hand-drawn-faq] On the other side, The Vinyl Room's 2023 roundup found many listeners say translucent vinyl sounds almost the same as black, while some advise avoiding white; it gives no measured figures in its free section.[^prs-vinylroom] So: a plant says the risk is real and manageable, listeners report anything from no difference to a noisy white disc, and nobody we read has measured it.

**Splatter** comes from mixing pellets of different colors in one biscuit; plants also hand-mix biscuits for color-in-color and A-side/B-side effects.[^prs-vinylmoon][^prs-vf-splatter]

### How is a picture disc made?

According to a UK retailer's blog, a **picture disc** is a PVC core with a printed paper or card image on each side, and the grooves are pressed into a thin clear polyethylene foil; the layers are pressed together and trimmed.[^prs-atlas] The same blog says the foil wears faster than regular vinyl and that picture discs tend to cost more.[^prs-atlas] A Los Angeles CD, vinyl and merch company's guide (2022, updated 2023) describes a PVC base with a piece of artwork on each side and a clear PVC layer over both, pressed between the stampers and trimmed.[^gf-b-unified] It calls the outer layer clear PVC and mentions no foil, and neither source says it presses records itself, so the outer layer's material is not settled in our sources.

### Are there eco-friendly records that aren't PVC?

There are, and every performance figure so far comes from the makers or the trade press, not independent tests.

- **EcoRecord.** Sonopress in Gutersloh, Germany, injection-molds recyclable PET instead of compression-molding PVC, on a machine of up to 300 tons, with no gas or steam; it claims energy savings of up to 85% in a test operation.[^prs-fvtp] It launched with Warner Music, and the first order was an orange Liam Gallagher and John Squire release listed at US $44.01 (2024, official retail price).[^prs-fvtp] The From Vinyl to Plastic writer was skeptical of the sound claims and asked for an independent review.[^prs-fvtp] Owsinski, writing in Hypebot in 2025, reported surface noise lower by up to 10 dB, but also visible wear after about 35 plays versus about 50 for standard vinyl, and a higher cost.[^prs-hypebot] At Making Vinyl 2024, about 70% of each EcoRecord was said to come from bottles collected from a river in Guatemala in 2023, nine bottles per record.[^prs-copper]
- **Electric injection elsewhere.** Green Vinyl Records replaced conventional gas-powered presses with an electric injection-molding machine and PVC with virgin and recycled PET, per an equipment supplier's case study that gives no figures or dates.[^prs-movacolor]
- **Bio-plastic and recycled PVC.** Evolution Music in the UK has made a record from sugars and starches, and Elastic Stage is working on a similar idea.[^prs-hypebot] Beologic regrinds records with the labels punched out; its own research said seven in 10 listeners preferred the recycled-PVC pressings (company research).[^prs-copper]
- **The cost question.** At the 2024 conference MPO asked whether labels could absorb "25 to 30 percent more" for PVC alternatives.[^prs-copper]
- **The rules.** VRMA and the Vinyl Alliance offered six anti-greenwashing principles in 2024: truthful claims, a full life-cycle view, and evidence.[^prs-copper]

None of this is new in spirit. In the mid-1960s Emory Cook ran a vacuum "microfusion" process using micro-particles of vinyl, and its makers claimed lower noise.[^prs-wikiwand-press] [The planet](/planet/) covers the footprint numbers.
