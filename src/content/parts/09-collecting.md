## Collecting and grading records

**The short version:** Collectors chase first pressings, but the codes in the dead wax only get you part of the way, and collectors themselves disagree on what counts as a "second pressing".[^run-wiki-matrix] Condition is graded on a shared ladder from Mint to Poor; in the US and on Discogs that is usually the Goldmine scale, in the UK the Record Collector scale.[^run-jazzcollector][^run-atlas-grading] Discogs users added 114.2 million items to their collections in 2025, a company record, and Record Store Day has run every year since 2008.[^run-discogs-2025][^run-rsd-uk]

[[DIAGRAM-IDEA: A grading ladder from Mint down to Poor, each rung with a one-line sound description and a rough value bar (NM 100%, VG+ about 50%, VG about 25%) marked "rule of thumb, Glide Magazine".]]

### What makes a first pressing a first pressing?

A **first pressing** is a copy from the first production run, and Wikipedia is blunt that matrix data alone does not prove it: cut 1 may have been rejected, and reissues often restart at 1.[^run-wiki-matrix] The word gets stretched, too. Per Wikipedia, collectors may call any change of track list, running order, label design, typo or cover a "second pressing".[^run-wiki-matrix]

So collectors stack evidence. One collector's routine starts with Discogs and its photos, then dates the label design, reads the dead wax, measures the pressing ring on the label, and cross-checks specialist forums because Discogs has errors.[^run-vinyldiscovery] A shop guide adds the jacket: tip-on construction, inserts, copyright years and label addresses, and the barcode test, since barcodes were not widely used before the late 1970s.[^run-mrs] The [runout section](/runout/) covers the codes themselves, including why Columbia's 1A to 1F were all first cuttings.[^run-ap-columbia]

The most collected records are not old. In Discogs' 2025 figures, the most-collected releases included a 2015 180 gram reissue of Nirvana's Nevermind and a 2017 picture disc of Taylor Swift's Reputation, and none of the top releases predate 2012.[^run-discogs-2025] Plenty of people want the record, not the first one.

### How does record grading work?

The common US yardstick is the **Goldmine standard**.[^run-atlas-grading] The table gives its definitions as summarized by the Jazz Collector site, which quotes them.[^run-jazzcollector]

| Grade | As summarized by Jazz Collector |
|---|---|
| Mint (M) | "Absolutely perfect in every way - certainly never played, possibly even still sealed"; to be used sparingly, if at all |
| Near Mint (NM or M-) | "A nearly perfect record"; no obvious wear; jacket without creases, folds or seam splits |
| Very Good Plus (VG+) | Signs of careful play; "Record surfaces may show some slight signs of wear"; slight warps that do not affect the sound are acceptable |
| Very Good (VG) | "Surface noise will be evident upon playing, especially in soft passages", but does not overpower the music |
| Good (G) / Good Plus (G+) | Plays through without skipping, with significant noise and visible wear |
| Poor (P) / Fair (F) | "cracked, badly warped and won't play through without skipping or repeating" |

Goldmine's own guide, on an undated page opened in 2026, says Mint "should never be used as a grade unless more than one person agrees" and that Near Mint records are "shiny, with no visible defects."[^gf-e-goldmine-101] It describes VG+ as a record that, "except for a couple minor things, this would be Near Mint," and says a VG record "has surface noise, and some scratches may be audible."[^gf-e-goldmine-101] It puts VG at no more than 25% of the Near Mint price, Good, Good Plus and VG- at 10 to 15%, and Poor and Fair at 0 to 5%, and says most records are graded visually.[^gf-e-goldmine-101]

Two things trip up new buyers. Mint should be rare on any honest listing. And the in-between grades you see on listings are not all official. Glide Magazine says NM is "a true Goldmine grade" while M-, VG++ and VG- are not.[^run-glide] Goldmine's current guide does list VG- alongside Good and Good Plus, so the two sources disagree on that grade.[^gf-e-goldmine-101]

Grading is done by eye and by ear. A record can look clean and still crackle, which is why the VG definition is written in terms of what you hear in quiet passages.[^run-jazzcollector]

### Which grading scale: Goldmine or Record Collector?

There are two main scales, and they mostly line up. A UK shop blog describes the Record Collector scale as the UK standard and Goldmine as the one used more by US sellers and Discogs.[^run-atlas-grading] The visible difference is a grade called **Excellent**, which the same blog places at roughly Goldmine VG+.[^run-atlas-grading] If you buy from another country, ask which scale the seller means.

### What is a grade worth?

There is no official conversion, only rules of thumb. Glide Magazine's version: a VG+ copy is worth about 50% of the Near Mint price and a VG copy about 25%, with price guides using NM as the base.[^run-glide] Treat those as a seller's shorthand, not a law; rarity, demand and the jacket move prices too.

### What does Discogs actually know?

**Discogs** launched in November 2000, started by Intel programmer Kevin Lewandowski, and is operated by Zink Media, LLC; per Wikipedia it holds over 19 million user-submitted releases as of 2026.[^run-wiki-discogs] Its collection figures are the company's own. In its 2025 report, Discogs said users added 114.2 million items to their collections that year, a record, up from 105.7 million in 2024; more than 920 million items were catalogued in all; the average collection held 191 items, with an average estimated value of $571.77 in the fourth quarter of 2025.[^run-discogs-2025] The company projected the total would pass one billion in 2026.[^run-discogs-2025]

Its 2025 Year in Review named The Beatles global artist of the year, Taylor Swift's The Life Of A Showgirl global album, and Columbia the top label.[^run-discogs-yir]

Marketplace figures are older. In December 2017, Resident Advisor reported over 37 million items for sale on the marketplace, about 27.5 million of them vinyl, and 130,000 orders in the first week of that December, a site record at the time.[^run-ra-discogs]

Discogs' own list of its most valuable sales from January 2020 to September 2023, published in September 2023, names Scaramanga Silk's "Choose Your Weapon" (2008) at $36,585 as the "most expensive item ever sold on Discogs."[^gf-e-discogs-valuable] We did not find a current official Discogs figure for annual marketplace sales or orders.

For identifying an exact copy, Discogs' database rules matter as much as its size: runouts are entered verbatim, one string per side, and minor differences can be added as variants.[^run-discogs-guide]

### What sells for big money on Discogs?

The press now reports Discogs' top sale month by month. Reported examples:

| Month | Record | Price | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| December 2025 | Misfits, "Bullet" 7-inch (1978) | $7,550 | 94.7 WCSX[^run-wcsx-misfits] |
| March 2026 | Pink Floyd, The Dark Side of the Moon | $11,842 | What Hi-Fi?[^run-whathifi-march] |
| June 2026 | The White Stripes, "Lafayette Blues" 7-inch | $25,000 | Loudwire[^run-loudwire-stripes] |

Loudwire called the White Stripes copy the top Discogs sale of 2026 to late July, one of only 15 known copies, from an edition with hand-painted covers.[^run-loudwire-stripes] Per Wikipedia's list of most valuable records, Prince's The Black Album sold for $27,500 on Discogs in 2018, and Scaramanga Silk's "Choose Your Weapon" sold there for $41,095 in an unstated year.[^run-wiki-valuable] That price differs from the $36,585 Discogs gives for the same record, and we have not resolved which is right.

### What is the most expensive record ever sold?

The vinyl answer, per Guinness World Records, is Ringo Starr's mono copy of The Beatles' White Album, numbered 0000001, which sold for $790,000 (£522,438) at Julien's in Los Angeles on December 5, 2015.[^run-guinness-white][^run-rollingstone-white]

Wikipedia's list adds other reported sales: the Elvis Presley "My Happiness" acetate for $300,000 in 2015, bought by Jack White; a signed Sgt. Pepper's for $290,500 in 2013; Frank Wilson's "Do I Love You (Indeed I Do)" 7-inch for $133,818 in 2020; a sealed Capitol US Yesterday and Today for $125,000 in 2016; and an Aphex Twin Caustic Window test pressing for $46,300 on eBay in 2014, bought by Minecraft creator Markus Persson.[^run-wiki-valuable]

The biggest number on that list is not vinyl. Wu-Tang Clan's Once Upon a Time in Shaolin sold for $4,000,000 in 2021, after an earlier $2 million sale in 2015, and it is a CD.[^run-wiki-valuable] The Quarrymen's "That'll Be the Day" acetate, owned by Paul McCartney, appears with a guide valuation of £200,000, which is an estimate, not a sale.[^run-wiki-valuable]

### What is Record Store Day?

**Record Store Day** grew out of a 2007 gathering of US record-shop owners, and the first official day was April 19, 2008, according to the UK organizers; the UK joined in 2008, brought over by Spencer Hickman, then at Rough Trade.[^run-rsd-uk] Wikipedia gives both 2007 and 2008 as the founding year, lists the founders as Eric Levin, Michael Kurtz, Carrie Colliton, Amy Dorfman, Brian Poehner and Don Van Cleave, and says Metallica launched the first event at Rasputin Music in Mountain View, California.[^run-wiki-rsd]

It grew fast: about 300 US stores in 2008, over 1,000 worldwide in 2009 and 1,400 in 2010, per Wikipedia.[^run-wiki-rsd] A Black Friday edition launched in November 2010, and in 2020 the day was split into three "Drops".[^run-wiki-rsd]

The UK rules show how it is meant to work. Releases are sold only in participating shops on the day, with no reservations or pre-orders, first come, first served, and no online sales until 8pm on the Monday after (April 20 in 2026); shops that break the rules can be banned.[^run-rsd-uk] The UK site lists 314 shops in the UK and Ireland and more than 500 releases, and the organizers say "more than 95%" of stock reaches fans rather than resellers.[^run-rsd-uk]

### Is Record Store Day good for records?

Both sides have numbers. Billboard reported, from Nielsen Music, that US vinyl sales rose from 990,000 units in 2007 to more than 13 million in 2016, and that the 2015 Record Store Day week was independent stores' best in 12 years, with scans up 46.7% on the week before.[^run-billboard-rsd] Wikipedia credits RSD 2024 with the highest weekly vinyl sales in 30 years.[^run-wiki-rsd]

Luminate, the data company formerly known as Nielsen Music, put the 2025 Record Store Day week (April 12) at 1.2 million albums sold, just over 1 million of them vinyl, and counted about 673,000 RSD-exclusive albums and vinyl singles bought at independent stores, which it called more than 75% of the units made for the day.[^gf-e-luminate-rsd-2025] The same 2025 post says 32% of US vinyl buyers surveyed attended and bought at Record Store Day in 2024.[^gf-e-luminate-rsd-2025] For 2026 (April 18), Billboard's Luminate chart report named Pink Floyd's Live From the Los Angeles Sports Arena, April 26th, 1975 the top-selling album of the week, with no total given.[^gf-e-billboard-rsd-2026]

Wikipedia's Record Store Day article lists criticisms raised in the UK: the day crowds pressing capacity, major labels are heavily involved, shops buy on no-return terms, and limited releases get flipped on resale sites.[^run-wiki-rsd] Whether that is a fair price for the attention it brings to shops is a question the article leaves open, and so do we.
