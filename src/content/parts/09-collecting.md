## Collecting and grading records

**The short version:** Collectors chase first pressings, but the codes in the dead wax only get you part of the way, and collectors can't even agree on what counts as a "second pressing".[^run-wiki-matrix] Condition is graded on a shared ladder from Mint to Poor: the Goldmine scale in the US and on Discogs, the Record Collector scale in the UK.[^run-jazzcollector][^run-atlas-grading] Discogs users added 114.2 million items to their collections in 2025, a company record, and Record Store Day has run every year since 2008.[^run-discogs-2025][^run-rsd-uk]

[[DIAGRAM-IDEA: A grading ladder from Mint down to Poor, each rung with a one-line sound description and a rough value bar (NM 100%, VG+ about 50%, VG about 25%) marked "rule of thumb, Glide Magazine".]]

### What makes a first pressing a first pressing?

A **first pressing** is a copy from the first production run, and matrix data alone won't prove you have one: cut 1 may have been rejected, and reissues often restart at 1.[^run-wiki-matrix] The term stretches, too. Collectors may call any change of track list, running order, label design, typo or cover a "second pressing".[^run-wiki-matrix]

So collectors stack evidence. One collector's routine starts with Discogs and its photos, then dates the label design, reads the dead wax, measures the pressing ring on the label, and cross-checks specialist forums, because Discogs has errors.[^run-vinyldiscovery] A shop guide adds the jacket: tip-on construction, inserts, copyright years, label addresses, and the barcode test, since barcodes were not widely used before the late 1970s.[^run-mrs] [Reading the runout](/runout/) covers the codes themselves, including why Columbia's 1A to 1F were all first cuttings.[^run-ap-columbia]

The most collected records are not old. In Discogs' 2025 figures, the most-collected releases included a 2015 180 gram reissue of Nirvana's Nevermind and a 2017 picture disc of Taylor Swift's Reputation, and none of the top releases predate 2012.[^run-discogs-2025] Plenty of people want the record, not the first one.

### How does record grading work?

The common US yardstick is the **Goldmine standard**.[^run-atlas-grading] Top to bottom:[^run-jazzcollector]

| Grade | What it means |
|---|---|
| Mint (M) | "Absolutely perfect in every way - certainly never played, possibly even still sealed"; to be used sparingly, if at all |
| Near Mint (NM or M-) | "A nearly perfect record"; no obvious wear; jacket without creases, folds or seam splits |
| Very Good Plus (VG+) | Signs of careful play; "Record surfaces may show some slight signs of wear"; slight warps that do not affect the sound are acceptable |
| Very Good (VG) | "Surface noise will be evident upon playing, especially in soft passages", but does not overpower the music |
| Good (G) / Good Plus (G+) | Plays through without skipping, with significant noise and visible wear |
| Poor (P) / Fair (F) | "cracked, badly warped and won't play through without skipping or repeating" |

Goldmine's own guide says Mint "should never be used as a grade unless more than one person agrees" and that Near Mint records are "shiny, with no visible defects."[^gf-e-goldmine-101] VG+ is a record that, "except for a couple minor things, this would be Near Mint," and a VG record "has surface noise, and some scratches may be audible."[^gf-e-goldmine-101] On price, it puts VG at no more than 25% of Near Mint, Good, Good Plus and VG- at 10 to 15%, and Poor and Fair at 0 to 5%, and it notes that most records are graded visually.[^gf-e-goldmine-101]

Two things trip up new buyers. Mint should be rare on any honest listing. And the in-between grades on listings are not all official. Glide Magazine calls NM "a true Goldmine grade" and says M-, VG++ and VG- are not,[^run-glide] though Goldmine's current guide lists VG- right alongside Good and Good Plus.[^gf-e-goldmine-101]

Grading is done by eye and by ear. A record can look clean and still crackle, which is why the VG definition is written in terms of what you hear in quiet passages.[^run-jazzcollector]

### Which grading scale: Goldmine or Record Collector?

There are two main scales, and they mostly line up. The Record Collector scale is the UK standard; Goldmine is the one most US sellers and Discogs use.[^run-atlas-grading] The visible difference is a grade called **Excellent**, which sits at roughly Goldmine VG+.[^run-atlas-grading] If you buy from another country, ask which scale the seller means.

### What is a grade worth?

There is no official conversion, only rules of thumb. Glide Magazine's version: a VG+ copy is worth about 50% of the Near Mint price and a VG copy about 25%, with price guides using NM as the base.[^run-glide] That is seller's shorthand, not law; rarity, demand and the jacket move prices too.

### What does Discogs actually know?

**Discogs** launched in November 2000, started by Intel programmer Kevin Lewandowski, and is operated by Zink Media, LLC. It holds over 19 million user-submitted releases as of 2026.[^run-wiki-discogs] The collection numbers are its own count. Users added 114.2 million items to their collections in 2025, a record, up from 105.7 million in 2024; more than 920 million items were catalogued in all; the average collection held 191 items, with an average estimated value of $571.77 in the fourth quarter of 2025.[^run-discogs-2025] Discogs expects the total to pass one billion in 2026.[^run-discogs-2025]

Its 2025 Year in Review named The Beatles global artist of the year, Taylor Swift's The Life Of A Showgirl global album, and Columbia the top label.[^run-discogs-yir]

Marketplace figures are older. In December 2017 the marketplace had over 37 million items for sale, about 27.5 million of them vinyl, and took 130,000 orders in the first week of that December, a site record at the time.[^run-ra-discogs]

Discogs' list of its most valuable sales from January 2020 to September 2023 names Scaramanga Silk's "Choose Your Weapon" (2008), at $36,585, the "most expensive item ever sold on Discogs."[^gf-e-discogs-valuable] Discogs has not published a current figure for annual marketplace sales or orders.

For pinning down an exact copy, the database rules matter as much as its size: runouts are entered verbatim, one string per side, and minor differences can be added as variants.[^run-discogs-guide]

### What sells for big money on Discogs?

The music press now reports Discogs' top sale month by month:

| Month | Record | Price | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| December 2025 | Misfits, "Bullet" 7-inch (1978) | $7,550 | 94.7 WCSX[^run-wcsx-misfits] |
| March 2026 | Pink Floyd, The Dark Side of the Moon | $11,842 | What Hi-Fi?[^run-whathifi-march] |
| June 2026 | The White Stripes, "Lafayette Blues" 7-inch | $25,000 | Loudwire[^run-loudwire-stripes] |

The White Stripes copy was the top Discogs sale of 2026 through late July, one of only 15 known copies, from an edition with hand-painted covers.[^run-loudwire-stripes] Prince's The Black Album sold for $27,500 on Discogs in 2018, and one widely cited list puts "Choose Your Weapon" at $41,095 there, year unstated[^run-wiki-valuable] - not the $36,585 Discogs itself gives for the record.

### What is the most expensive record ever sold?

On vinyl, Guinness gives it to Ringo Starr's mono copy of The Beatles' White Album, numbered 0000001, which sold for $790,000 (£522,438) at Julien's in Los Angeles on December 5, 2015.[^run-guinness-white][^run-rollingstone-white]

Below it sit other reported sales: the Elvis Presley "My Happiness" acetate for $300,000 in 2015, bought by Jack White; a signed Sgt. Pepper's for $290,500 in 2013; Frank Wilson's "Do I Love You (Indeed I Do)" 7-inch for $133,818 in 2020; a sealed Capitol US Yesterday and Today for $125,000 in 2016; and an Aphex Twin Caustic Window test pressing for $46,300 on eBay in 2014, bought by Minecraft creator Markus Persson.[^run-wiki-valuable]

The biggest number of all is a CD. Wu-Tang Clan's Once Upon a Time in Shaolin sold for $4,000,000 in 2021, after an earlier $2 million sale in 2015.[^run-wiki-valuable] The Quarrymen's "That'll Be the Day" acetate, owned by Paul McCartney, carries a guide valuation of £200,000, which is an estimate, not a sale.[^run-wiki-valuable]

### What is Record Store Day?

**Record Store Day** grew out of a 2007 gathering of US record-shop owners, and the UK organizers date the first official day to April 19, 2008; the UK joined that same year, brought over by Spencer Hickman, then at Rough Trade.[^run-rsd-uk] Other accounts give either 2007 or 2008 as the founding year.[^run-wiki-rsd] The founders were Eric Levin, Michael Kurtz, Carrie Colliton, Amy Dorfman, Brian Poehner and Don Van Cleave, and Metallica launched the first event at Rasputin Music in Mountain View, California.[^run-wiki-rsd]

It grew fast: about 300 US stores in 2008, over 1,000 worldwide in 2009 and 1,400 in 2010.[^run-wiki-rsd] A Black Friday edition launched in November 2010, and in 2020 the day was split into three "Drops".[^run-wiki-rsd]

The UK rules show how it is meant to work. Releases are sold only in participating shops on the day, with no reservations or pre-orders, first come, first served, and no online sales until 8pm on the Monday after (April 20 in 2026); shops that break the rules can be banned.[^run-rsd-uk] The UK site lists 314 shops in the UK and Ireland and more than 500 releases, and the organizers say "more than 95%" of stock reaches fans rather than resellers.[^run-rsd-uk]

### Is Record Store Day good for records?

Both sides have numbers. US vinyl sales rose from 990,000 units in 2007 to more than 13 million in 2016, by Nielsen Music's count, and the 2015 Record Store Day week was independent stores' best in 12 years, with scans up 46.7% on the week before.[^run-billboard-rsd] RSD 2024 brought the highest weekly vinyl sales in 30 years.[^run-wiki-rsd]

Luminate, the data company formerly known as Nielsen Music, put the 2025 Record Store Day week (April 12) at 1.2 million albums sold, just over 1 million of them vinyl, and counted about 673,000 RSD-exclusive albums and vinyl singles bought at independent stores - more than 75% of the units made for the day.[^gf-e-luminate-rsd-2025] Of the US vinyl buyers it surveyed, 32% attended and bought at Record Store Day in 2024.[^gf-e-luminate-rsd-2025] The top seller of the 2026 week (April 18) was Pink Floyd's Live From the Los Angeles Sports Arena, April 26th, 1975; no total for the week has been published.[^gf-e-billboard-rsd-2026]

The UK complaints are familiar ones: the day crowds pressing capacity, major labels are heavily involved, shops buy on no-return terms, and limited releases get flipped on resale sites.[^run-wiki-rsd] Whether that is a fair price for the foot traffic it brings to shops is still argued.
