The short version: DJs turned the record player into an instrument. A Japanese direct-drive turntable launched in 1972 became the club standard, scratching came out of the Bronx in the mid-1970s (exactly when, and who was first, is still argued), and since the early 2000s timecode vinyl has let DJs keep a record under their hand while playing digital files.

How did a hi-fi turntable end up in every club?#

Matsushita, now Panasonic, launched the Technics SL-1200 in October 1972, developed the year before by a team led by Shuichi Obata.143 It used direct drive, with the platter sitting on the motor (drive types are covered in playing records). For DJs that meant high torque, a near-instant start and steady speed with less wow and flutter, and a platter that could be pushed around by hand or spun backward without harm.135 Belt drives were less consistent and prone to failure, so in the belt era DJs developed the slip cue (hold the record still while the platter turns, then let go), which works better on direct drive anyway. As IEEE Spectrum puts it, "Scratching was impossible on belt drives, because it would destroy them."135

Technics says that in August 1973 DJ Kool Herc used "a pair of Technics direct drive turntables" in the Bronx for his breakbeat technique - a maker's story, and it doesn't name the model.136 The 1979 SL-1200MK2 is billed by Technics as "the first turntable specifically developed for use in discos,"136 and its main change was braking that matched its acceleration.143 As the club scene grew through the 1980s, the series became the de facto standard turntable in clubs.136 An SL-1210MK2 is on display at London's Science Museum.143 IEEE Spectrum calls the direct-drive turntable "the only music-playback device to become a musical instrument itself."135

How many were sold?#

Nobody independent has counted. Technics says 3.5 million units "over the past 35 years," without saying when those 35 years ended.136 The other figure in circulation, "more than 3 million units sold," traces back to Technics too.143 Both are the maker's own cumulative totals, not audited sales.

When did it stop, and when did it come back?#

Panasonic announced the end of Technics analog turntables on November 1, 2010, through the DMC World DJ Championships home page,143 and IEEE Spectrum counts 38 years of production ending that year.135 At CES 2016 the deck returned as the SL-1200G and a limited SL-1200GAE of 1,200 units; the GAE shipped in April 2016 and the G in October, at a reported launch price of about USD 4,000 / GBP 3,100 / EUR 3,499.143 The SL-1200MK7 followed at CES 2019,143 which Technics calls "a full revival of the feel of the SL-1200MK2."136 Production is said to have moved from Japan to Malaysia in 2021, though the evidence for that is thin.143

What do the numbers on a DJ deck mean?#

The MK7 is a fair modern reference. Panasonic lists:137

Spec SL-1200MK7 (maker figure)
Wow and flutter 0.025% weighted RMS
Start-up to 33 1/3 rpm 0.7 s
Starting torque 0.18 N·m
Pitch range up to ±16%
Speeds 33 1/3, 45, 78 rpm
Effective arm length 230 mm
Weight about 9.6 kg, with a 1.8 kg platter

Pitch control is the fader that speeds the platter up or slows it down to match tempos. The classic range was ±8%, and the MK5G, released in 2002, added a ±16% button and was the first Technics deck with digitally controlled pitch.143

Who invented scratching, and when?#

The usual story: Grand Wizzard Theodore (Theodore Livingston), about 12 years old in the Bronx, found it by accident, moving a record back and forth while his mother told him to turn the music down.144 KEXP dates that discovery to 1975 and his public debut to 1977 at the Sparkle club, with Grandmaster Flash and Theodore's older brother, Mean Gene, as mentors.144 KEXP is careful to add that it is "difficult to attribute specific people or dates": he "might not be the first person" to rock a record back and forth, only the first known to see what it could do.144

Push on the date and it slides around. The Vinyl Factory puts the Theodore story in the "early 1980s" and calls it legend.145 IEEE Spectrum has club DJs scratching in the "early 1970s."135

Is turntablism older than hip-hop?#

Records as instruments go back further. John Cage's Imaginary Landscape No. 1 (1939) used records switched between speeds, Pierre Schaeffer's Etude aux Chemins de Fer (1948) was built from manipulated recordings of steam locomotives, and Cage's 33 1/3 (1969) was written for 12 record players.145 DMC has run its annual World DJ Championships since 1985, and in 1995 DJ Babu of the Beat Junkies coined the word turntablism.145 Technics dates the concept of turntablism to the 1990s.136

How does timecode vinyl work?#

A DVS (digital vinyl system) swaps the music on the record for a control signal. The turntable's output goes into an audio interface or mixer, software reads the signal for speed, direction and position, plays a digital file to match, and sends that audio back to the mixer like any other deck.146 There is always some latency, and lower is better.146

Open-source developers on the Mixxx project have taken the signals apart. Serato's control record carries a 1,000 Hz tone with the right channel shifted a quarter cycle (90 degrees) against the left; comparing the two at each zero crossing tells forward from backward, and counting crossings, 2,000 a second at normal speed, gives speed.147 In relative mode the software uses only speed and direction; in absolute mode it also reads where the needle is, so dropping the needle further in jumps the track.147 Position comes from bits riding on the carrier: Serato uses amplitude modulation and a 20-bit sequence with up to 1,048,575 states, while Traktor's MK2 timecode uses a 2,500 Hz carrier, about 2.5 times Serato's resolution, and a 110-bit sequence.148 These are reverse-engineered findings, not vendor specs.

Where did DVS come from?#

Chris Bauer's Spacedeck project dates to 1998, and Final Scratch, released in 2001, was "the first DVS software sold publicly."146 Serato began in 1997, when Steve West was at the University of Auckland in New Zealand and wrote a pitch and tempo algorithm, and Serato Scratch Live came out with Rane in 2004.149 Native Instruments' Traktor Scratch reached stores worldwide in May 2007 at a suggested USD 669 / EUR 599, with a cheaper crossgrade for owners of FinalScratch and Serato Scratch Live.150 In 2011 the DMC championships began allowing vinyl-emulation systems.145 For how turntables, CDJs and DVS rigs sit together in a working club booth, see https://showfile.cold.fyi/.

Why do DJs still buy records?#

No one has published a survey of why working DJs still buy vinyl, so the answer stays short. Direct-drive decks were built to be handled, pushed and reversed,135 DVS exists to keep that hand-on-platter control while the music comes from files,146 and Technics judged demand strong enough to restart production in 2016 and add the MK7 in 2019.143

Published sales data is scarce and mostly about hardware. Citing MI Salestrak figures, DJ TechTools reported that turntables took about 14% of US unit sales of DJ technology in May 2016, against 6.3% for CDJs and 42% for controllers.151 On the record side (see what a record costs), Resident Advisor reported in 2023 that pressing costs and plant delays had labels and shops asking whether the dance 12-inch was on the way out; one label cofounder said pressing 300 records had gone from little more than GBP 1,000 to close to GBP 2,000, and 12-inch singles from about GBP 9 to GBP 14.152

Quick answers

Who invented scratching?
Grand Wizzard Theodore in the Bronx is usually credited. KEXP dates his discovery to 1975 and his public debut to 1977, other sources give the early 1980s or early 1970s, and KEXP notes he may not have been the first.
How does Serato timecode vinyl work?
The control record carries a 1,000 Hz tone with the right channel 90 degrees behind the left, which tells the software speed and direction. Position bits on the tone let absolute mode know where the needle is; open-source developers worked this out by reverse-engineering it.
