Vinyl and the planet

On this side

The short version: Most records are pressed from PVC (polyvinyl chloride), a petroleum-based plastic. Kyle Devine of the University of Oslo estimates the US recording industry used far less plastic in 2016 than at the vinyl peak of 1977, but produced more greenhouse gas, because storing and streaming digital files takes energy too. Makers are trying bio-based PVC, bioplastics and injection molding; the gains they report are their own claims, not independent measurements.

What is a record made of, and how is it pressed?#

A conventional LP starts as a lump of PVC called a biscuit (or puck), placed between two stampers in a steam-heated press and squeezed flat (how pressing works).187 On a manual press one cycle takes about 25 seconds.187 Heat, then cooling, then the next one.

The plastic itself is a commodity with a price that moves. One plant owner said in 2022 that the price of PVC had gone up three times since his plant opened in 2020.177 That lands on the cost of every record, and it is part of why some plants have looked at other materials.

Is vinyl worse for the planet than streaming?#

It depends what you count. The best-known numbers cover the whole US recording industry, not any single record. Kyle Devine of the University of Oslo estimated its plastic use and greenhouse gas emissions at four points:179

Year Plastic used (US recording industry) Greenhouse gas
1977 58 million kg 140 million kg
1988 56 million kg 136 million kg
2000 61 million kg 157 million kg
2016 about 8 million kg an estimated 200 to over 350 million kg

Academic estimate (Kyle Devine), published 2019.179

Plastic fell by more than 85% between 1977 and 2016, while estimated emissions rose, with the 2016 figure attributed to storing and transmitting digital files.179

Physical formats were never the whole footprint, and streaming isn't weightless. But 2016 is one year in a format cycle that keeps moving, and industry totals can't tell you what one LP costs the planet against a month of streaming.

The study had an economic half, by Matt Brennan: recorded music fell from about 4.83% of an average US weekly salary in 1977 to about 1.22% in 2013.179

The industry's first carbon footprint report, published in June 2024 by the Vinyl Record Manufacturers Association and the Vinyl Alliance, put a record at about 1.15 kg CO2e on average, measured cradle to factory gate at one pressing plant over 2022-2023.188 For 12-inch LPs it ranged from about 1.07 kg at 140 grams to about 1.28 kg at 200 grams, with PVC compound about half the total and factory energy about 30%.188 The report calls the figure partial and leaves out distribution, use and disposal; it is the industry measuring itself, not an independent audit.188

What is BioVinyl?#

BioVinyl is still PVC, made differently. optimal media in Germany189 launched it with PlastChem in July 2023: the petroleum feedstock in the PVC is replaced with recycled used cooking oil or industrial waste gases.190 That makes it bio-attributed (or bio-based) PVC, and the supply chain is certified under ISCC PLUS, an independent certification standard.190

optimal media says the records are "acoustically and optically identical" to conventional vinyl, come in black plus 11 colors, and carry a "significantly reduced" CO2 footprint - though it puts no number on the reduction.190

Can a record be made without PVC at all?#

Two companies say yes, by different routes.

Evolution Music, a UK company led by CEO Marc Carey, presses records from a bioplastic whose recipe includes sugars and starches, not PVC.191 The company said the records would cost about the same as vinyl at full production.191 On sound, one producer put it at "Maybe 95%" of vinyl191 - a listener's guess, not a measurement.

Green Vinyl Records, in the Eindhoven region of the Netherlands, came out of the CD and DVD industry as a project of the Symcon Group.192 Instead of steam presses, it uses injection molding, forcing molten material into a mold, with its own non-PVC compounds, and prints directly on the disc instead of using paper labels.192 It claims 93% less energy than conventional steam pressing, by its own measurement.192

Approach Who Material Press Claimed benefit Status of claim
Bio-attributed PVC optimal media with PlastChem, 2023 PVC from used cooking oil or waste gases Not stated "Significantly reduced" CO2 Manufacturer190
Bioplastic Evolution Music, 2022 Sugar and starch recipe Not stated Similar cost at full production Company, reported191
Injection molding Green Vinyl Records, 2021 Non-PVC compounds Injection molding 93% less energy Company's own measurement192

Do these records sound as good?#

That is the contested part. optimal media says BioVinyl is acoustically identical;190 one producer gave Evolution Music's bioplastic "Maybe 95%."191 Nobody has published an independent listening test or lab comparison, so the verdict is down to your ears.

What about shipping, jackets and shrinkwrap?#

A record also travels: plant to distributor to shop to home, in board, paper and plastic film. The 2024 industry report counts packaging inside its 1.15 kg figure: print packaging (jackets, inserts, sleeves) was about 13% of the footprint and transport packaging about 3%.188 A printed single jacket came to about 0.10-0.15 kg CO2e and a gatefold to 0.20-0.30 kg, while shrinkwrap was about 8 grams CO2e.188 Freight sits outside the figure, and the report's own examples show why it matters: Prague to Melbourne adds about 0.1 kg a record by sea and about 3.46 kg by air, and the report calls international air freight "the elephant in the room."188

What can a buyer or an artist do?#

With one industry estimate per record and no independent ones, the list is short. Artists pressing a run can ask a plant which materials and certifications it offers and what evidence sits behind any environmental claim. Buyers can weigh how often they will play a record against how many editions of it they are tempted to own. The pricing side of those choices is in what a record costs.
